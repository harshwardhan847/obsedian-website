The fact that you got **650 waitlist signups from one Instagram video** is the most valuable signal you have right now. Before spending time on Product Hunt or polishing every detail, double down on the channel that has already shown traction.

The goal is:

> **Get as many qualified people onto your waitlist as possible and build anticipation for launch.**

I would think of this in **3 phases**.

# Metrics to Track

Don't obsess over followers.

Track these instead:

- Waitlist signups/day
    
- Cost per signup (if you run ads later)
    
- Landing page conversion rate
    
- Reel views → waitlist conversion
    
- Referral rate
    
- Email open rate
    
- Comments and DMs asking "When is this launching?"
    

Those metrics tell you whether interest is translating into intent.

# What I would do if I were launching AutoApply

**Week 1**

- Publish 2 Instagram Reels/day.
    
- Cross-post every Reel to YouTube Shorts.
    
- Publish 5–8 tweets/day showing progress, demos, and lessons learned.
    
- Write 2 LinkedIn posts about the problem and your journey.
    
- Set up a referral waitlist so users can move up by inviting friends.
    
- Email every new signup immediately with their position and what's next.
    

**Week 2**

- Continue daily short-form content.
    
- Release one longer YouTube video explaining the problem and showing the product.
    
- Become active on Product Hunt by supporting other launches and engaging with makers.
    
- Collect testimonials from early testers and turn them into content.
    

**Launch week**

- Build anticipation with a daily countdown.
    
- Coordinate announcements across Instagram, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, email, and Product Hunt so they all point to the same landing page.
    
- Spend the entire Product Hunt launch day responding to comments, answering questions, and encouraging discussion.
    

One final suggestion: your first Instagram Reel already proved something important. Instead of trying to invent new content formats, **analyze exactly why that Reel worked**—its hook, pacing, visuals, and CTA—and create 10–20 variations around the same theme. When a channel is already producing signups, it's usually more effective to amplify that winning pattern than to constantly search for new ideas.
